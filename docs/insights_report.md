# PharmaPulse Insights Report

## 1. Executive Summary

The clearest commercial opportunity in this analysis is improving prioritization quality: high-value HCPs, high-efficiency territories, and high-margin products do not always align with activity volume alone.

The PharmaPulse analysis indicates uneven commercial performance across territories, reps, products, HCP segments, therapy areas, and campaigns. The strongest directional signals are territory efficiency gaps, mixed rep target achievement, margin-led product prioritization, HCP recency risk, therapy-area call efficiency variation, and campaign response quality differences.

Overall, the findings suggest that commercial planning should not rely on activity volume or revenue alone. A stronger operating view would combine INR revenue, sales targets, margin contribution, call efficiency, HCP tier, engagement score, call recency, and campaign response quality.

## 2. Key Insights

### Insight 1: Territory efficiency varies despite similar HCP coverage

- **Observation:** Territory revenue differs meaningfully even though most territories have similar HCP coverage.
- **Evidence:** Pune Central generated ₹204.5M net sales from 42 unique HCPs and 1,972 calls, equal to approximately ₹103.7K revenue per call. Ahmedabad Gujarat generated ₹119.6M from 41 unique HCPs and 1,496 calls, or approximately ₹79.9K per call. Gurgaon Corporate generated lower total revenue than Pune at ₹153.6M, but had the highest revenue per call at approximately ₹109.0K.
- **Business Meaning:** This suggests territory productivity is not only a coverage question. HCP mix, product mix, territory potential, and field execution may all require review.
- **Recommendation:** Track territory-level revenue, HCP coverage, call volume, and revenue per call together when setting targets and allocating field effort.

### Insight 2: Rep target achievement shows clear coaching segments

- **Observation:** Rep performance shows over-target, near-target, and under-target segments, with efficiency differences that are not fully explained by call volume.
- **Evidence:** Dalbir Sodhi delivered ₹42.7M net sales against a ₹28.9M target, reaching 147.9% achievement. Aahana Chokshi delivered ₹39.4M against a ₹55.9M target, reaching 70.5%. Near-target reps include Tanish Grover at 100.8% and Udant Gill at 99.1%. Tanish Grover also had the highest revenue per call at approximately ₹127.2K, while Nicholas Seth reached 106.3% of target despite a lower revenue per call of approximately ₹71.2K.
- **Business Meaning:** Target achievement and call efficiency provide different coaching signals. Some reps may need opportunity quality review, while others may be performing well against appropriately sized targets.
- **Recommendation:** Segment reps by target achievement and revenue per call to guide coaching, recognition, and territory support.

### Insight 3: Product portfolio prioritization should be margin-led, not volume-led

- **Observation:** High unit volume and high revenue do not always translate into high margin contribution.
- **Evidence:** PainAway generated ₹216.3M net sales and ₹146.2M estimated margin contribution with a 67.61% margin. SugarNorm sold more units at 58,872 and generated ₹172.0M net sales, but produced lower estimated margin contribution at ₹90.2M. GlucoRight sold 53,893 units and generated ₹64.1M net sales, but its 23.2% margin translated to only ₹14.9M estimated margin contribution.
- **Business Meaning:** Volume-led prioritization may understate the value of higher-margin brands and overstate the commercial value of lower-margin products.
- **Recommendation:** Use margin contribution, net sales, units sold, and therapy area context together when setting product focus and promotion priorities.

### Insight 4: HCP prioritization should account for tier value and recency risk

- **Observation:** HCP tier is a strong revenue proxy, but call recency highlights additional relationship-management risk.
- **Evidence:** Tier 1 HCPs averaged ₹5.33M revenue per HCP, compared with ₹3.71M for Tier 2 and ₹2.41M for Tier 3. Average engagement scores were similar across tiers, ranging from 50.41 to 50.55. A recency check found 17 Tier 1 HCPs above the high-revenue threshold of ₹4.28M with at least 19 days since last call. One example is Dr. Dev Shetty in Mumbai Metro, a Tier 1 HCP with ₹9.85M net sales, 47 calls, a 51.32 engagement score, and 22 days since last call.
- **Business Meaning:** Revenue concentration suggests Tier 1 HCPs deserve close account planning, while stale call recency among high-value HCPs may require follow-up review.
- **Recommendation:** Prioritize HCPs using revenue, tier, engagement score, and days since last call rather than a single metric.

### Insight 5: Therapy areas show different revenue-per-call profiles

- **Observation:** Therapy areas with similar engagement scores can show very different revenue efficiency.
- **Evidence:** Respiratory generated ₹322.3M net sales across 2,275 calls, or approximately ₹141.7K revenue per call. Neurology generated ₹91.2M across 2,031 calls, or approximately ₹44.9K per call. Average engagement scores were directionally similar, with Respiratory at 49.80 and Neurology at 51.52.
- **Business Meaning:** Engagement score alone may not explain therapy-area performance. Product economics, patient volume, prescribing opportunity, and therapy-area maturity may influence revenue efficiency.
- **Recommendation:** Compare therapy areas using revenue, call volume, engagement score, and revenue per call before changing call allocation or brand emphasis.

### Insight 6: Campaign coverage is healthy, but response quality varies by type

- **Observation:** Campaign engagement coverage appears healthy, while engagement behavior differs more clearly through open rate, click rate, and attendance rate than through response score.
- **Evidence:** The lowest campaign engagement count was 18, and no campaigns were flagged as `NO ENGAGEMENTS` or `LOW ENGAGEMENT`. Field Event had the highest average click rate at 31.45%, while Email had the lowest average open rate at 69.23%. Response scores were relatively stable across campaign types, so they should be treated as a secondary signal.
- **Business Meaning:** Coverage does not appear to be the primary issue. Channel and campaign-type behavior may provide a clearer directional signal for message optimization than response score alone.
- **Recommendation:** Review campaign type performance using open rate, click rate, attendance rate, and response score together, with response score used as a supporting signal rather than the primary differentiator.

## 3. Recommendations

1. Build territory reviews around INR revenue, call volume, HCP coverage, revenue per call, and market potential.
2. Use target achievement and revenue per call together for rep coaching and performance segmentation.
3. Prioritize product focus using estimated margin contribution, not revenue or units alone.
4. Use HCP tier, revenue, engagement score, and call recency to guide call planning.
5. Compare therapy areas using revenue-per-call and engagement patterns before changing field effort.
6. Evaluate campaigns by response quality, open rate, click rate, and attendance rate in addition to engagement coverage.

## 4. Limitations

These findings are based on simulated commercial data and should be interpreted as directional patterns. A real-world deployment would incorporate market access, formulary, competitive activity, prescribing behavior, and field-force capacity data. Engagement score, response score, revenue per call, and estimated margin contribution are useful proxies, but they should be validated against real commercial context before operational decisions are made.

## 5. Next Steps

1. Add territory drilldowns that compare INR revenue, market potential, call volume, and revenue per call.
2. Create a rep coaching view that separates target achievement from call efficiency.
3. Add a product portfolio view that ranks brands by estimated margin contribution and therapy area.
4. Create an HCP prioritization score using tier, revenue, engagement score, and call recency.
5. Benchmark campaign types by response score, open rate, click rate, and attendance rate.
